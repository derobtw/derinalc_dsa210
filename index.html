The Structural Truth of Used-Car Prices

An empirical analysis of the Turkish automotive market. Testing whether color truly changes value, or whether price is overwhelmingly driven by structural vehicle characteristics.

Launch Valuation Tool
Project Scope

Data Collection & Enrichment

48,062 Data Points

Real-world used car listings were cleaned, standardized, and prepared for statistical testing and predictive modeling.

MTV Integration

The dataset was enriched with the official 2026 Motor Vehicle Tax (MTV) tariff from GİB to incorporate ownership-cost structure.

The Hypothesis

The project tests whether color meaningfully changes resale value after controlling for year, mileage, brand, transmission, and body type.

Analytical Approach

Methodology

EDA & Transformations

Exploratory analysis and log transformations were used to understand skewed price patterns and stabilize model behavior.

Hypothesis Testing

Nested regression and non-parametric testing showed that color adds only a tiny amount of explanatory power.

Machine Learning

Linear Regression and Random Forest models were compared. The strongest result came from the extended linear model.

Conclusion

Key Findings

1. The superficial gap: Raw averages suggest price differences among colors, but this impression weakens sharply after structural controls are included.

2. Structural dominance: Brand, year, mileage, transmission, and body type explain most of the value variation seen in the second-hand market.

3. Practical takeaway: Color may be statistically detectable, but it is not a major financial driver once structural vehicle features are taken into account.

"In the Turkish second-hand market, valuation is primarily structural. Cosmetic attributes are secondary."
